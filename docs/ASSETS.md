# Visual assets: provenance, layout and performance

Every image the game ships is generated by code in this repository. Nothing is traced, sampled,
photographed or downloaded; there are no third-party fonts, textures or sprite packs. The visual
direction (modern cinematic, navy/cyan interface, Arizona youth-soccer setting) is our own reading
of the approved brief; no Final Fantasy or other franchise assets are referenced or reproduced.

## Sprite sheet layout

Declared once in `src/render/spriteLayout.ts` and mirrored into `atlas.json`.

- Frame: **64 × 80 px**; figure height 68 px; anchor **(32, 74)** = the point between the feet.
  The renderer scales a frame so that 68 px equals the figure height on screen and places the anchor
  at the player's engine position projected to the screen.
- Columns (facing): `S, SE, E, NE, N` — five authored facings. `SW`, `W`, `NW` are the `SE`, `E`,
  `NE` frames mirrored horizontally at draw time (`MIRRORED` in `src/render/sprites.ts`), which is
  why kit trim and hair are symmetric.
- Rows: 5 poses × 3 looks = 15 rows, in the order `stand, run1, run2, ready, carry`, each with
  `look0..look2`. `stand` is the idle; `run1`/`run2` alternate with stride; `ready` is a low,
  wide goalkeeper/receiving stance; `carry` is a running frame with the ball-side foot leading.
- Looks: three skin/hair combinations, assigned deterministically from the player id so the same
  player always looks the same across replays and saves.
- Kits: `blue` and `coral` outfield, `keeperHome` and `keeperAway` for the goalkeepers (gloves
  included). The presentation adapter picks the kit from the side and role; kits are readable
  against the turf and against each other at the smallest supported zoom.

## Generation

`tools/spriteGen.ts` composes each frame from parametric shapes (`tools/sprites/figure.ts`:
head, torso, arms, legs, boots, hair, kit trim) rasterised with anti-aliased coverage by
`tools/sprites/raster.ts`, then encodes the sheet as a PNG (zlib via `node:zlib`, no external
encoder). The generator is deterministic — the same source produces byte-identical PNGs — and the
figures are deliberately youthful in proportion (large head, short torso) and stylised.

## Loading and fallback

`loadSprites()` in `src/render/sprites.ts` fetches the four sheets in parallel when the match
screen mounts, with a `LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS = 6000` per sheet. Each kit resolves independently: a sheet
that fails or times out is recorded in `SpriteSet.failed` and only that kit falls back to the
procedural figures in `src/render/figures.ts`; the match never waits for artwork. Tests:
`tests/render/sprites.test.ts` (direction mapping, mirroring, per-kit failure, timeout, renderer
fallback).

## Rendering contract

All figure visuals are derived from authoritative engine state by `src/render/presentation.ts`
(`deriveVisuals`): facing from velocity (or ball direction when still), pose from speed, ball
control and goalkeeper role, receiving flag from the ball's target, pressure from the nearest
opponent, kit from side/role. The renderer never reads `MatchState` directly for figure drawing
and never writes to it; sub-tick interpolation (`pos + vel × carry`) is display-only and the
deterministic replay of a seed is unchanged (`tests/render/presentation.test.ts`).

## Performance

Measured numbers live in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`. Summary for this build: a frame at a portrait
phone viewport costs ≈ 140 draw calls with sprites (≈ 200 when a sheet is missing), render path
p95 0.07 ms on the dev machine, 0 long frames over two full 60-minute matches; the four sheets add
≈ 400 kB of PNG fetched once and cached by the service worker.
